# Supplier Verification

Evidence checklist, verification signals, red flags vs. insufficient-evidence distinction, complaint/review analysis, and confidence reporting format.

---

## 1. PURPOSE

Verification answers: "Is this supplier real, reachable, and likely to deliver what they claim?" It does NOT answer "Is this supplier the cheapest?" or "Is this supplier the best quality?" — those are comparison questions.

Verification uses **public evidence only**. A supplier that cannot be verified is not necessarily fake — it means the public footprint is too thin to confirm.

---

## 2. EVIDENCE CHECKLIST

Check each item. Record what was found and where. "Not found" is a valid result — it contributes to grade D, not E.

### 2.1 Business identity

| Check | What to look for | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Legal name | Consistent across website, invoices, directories | Website, directory listings |
| Business registration | NIB (Nomor Induk Berusaha), TDP (older), NPWP, equivalent foreign registration | OSS system, ministry sites, company footer |
| Tax ID | NPWP mentioned on website/invoice templates | Website, sample invoices |
| Physical address | Street address, not just "Jakarta" | Website contact page, Google Maps |
| Years in business | "Established 2010" or domain age | Website about page, WHOIS |

### 2.2 Digital presence

| Check | What to look for | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Website | Professional, functional, current content | Direct URL |
| Domain age | >2 years = established; <1 year = new/unknown | WHOIS lookup |
| SSL certificate | HTTPS active (basic legitimacy signal) | Browser |
| Contact page | Phone, email, address, business hours | Website |
| Product catalog | Real products with specs, not stock photos only | Website |
| Social media | Active profiles on LinkedIn, Instagram, Facebook | Platform search |
| Content freshness | Recent posts, updated products, current events | Social media, blog |

### 2.3 Commercial signals

| Check | What to look for | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing transparency | Prices listed or "request quote" | Website, marketplace |
| MOQ stated | Minimum order quantity visible | Website, marketplace |
| Payment methods | Bank transfer, invoice, PO accepted | Website terms |
| Delivery info | Shipping zones, lead times, tracking | Website, marketplace |
| Warranty stated | Clear warranty terms | Website, product pages |
| Return policy | How returns/exchanges work | Website terms |

### 2.4 Third-party verification

| Check | What to look for | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Authorized seller badge | "Authorized dealer" / "Official store" | Marketplace, manufacturer site |
| Manufacturer listing | Brand's store locator or partner page | Brand website |
| Government supplier list | Listed as approved vendor | Ministry/Dinas site |
| Industry association | Member of relevant association | Association directory |
| Review platforms | Google Reviews, marketplace ratings, Trustpilot | Platform |
| News/press | Featured in media, won awards | News search |
| Portfolio/case studies | Documented projects, client names | Website |

### 2.5 Contact verification

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Phone reachable | Number format consistent with location |
| Email domain | Matches website domain or known business domain |
| Response time | (Only if user authorizes contact — engine does NOT contact) |
| Address on map | Street view exists, business signage visible |

## 3. VERIFICATION SIGNALS — POSITIVE INDICATORS

Strong signals that increase confidence:

| Signal | Weight | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Government registration verified | High | Legal entity exists |
| Authorized by manufacturer | High | Brand stands behind them |
| Physical store/showroom | Medium-High | Real-world presence |
| Multiple consistent contact channels | Medium | Reachable and accountable |
| Years of operation | Medium | Survival implies some legitimacy |
| Independent positive reviews | Medium | Third-party validation |
| Professional website with real content | Medium | Investment in legitimate business |
| Listed on B2B platform with verification | Medium | Platform did some vetting |
| Clear terms/warranty/policies | Low-Medium | Professional business practice |

## 4. RED FLAGS vs. INSUFFICIENT EVIDENCE

This distinction is critical. **A red flag is different from missing evidence.**

### 4.1 Red flags (E-grade signals)

These are **active negative signals**, not just absence of positive ones:

| Red flag | Description | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| Identity inconsistency | Name, address, or registration differs across sources | High |
| Unverifiable registration | Claims registration but can't be found in any registry | High |
| Scam reports | Multiple complaints of non-delivery, fraud | Critical |
| Domain impersonation | Website mimics a known brand without authorization | Critical |
| No real contact | Only a contact form, no phone/address, unreachable email | High |
| Suspicious pricing | 50%+ below market with no explanation | Medium-High |
| Pressure tactics | "Only today!" urgency, demands advance payment to personal account | High |
| No product specificity | Vague descriptions, stock photos only, no specs | Medium |
| Recently registered domain + no footprint | Brand new online presence claiming years in business | Medium-High |
| Fake reviews | Generic 5-star reviews with no detail, same phrasing | Medium |
| Unverifiable "authorized" claim | Says "authorized dealer" but brand doesn't list them | High |

### 4.2 Insufficient evidence (D-grade, NOT E)

These are **neutral gaps** — information that couldn't be found, not information that contradicts:

| Gap | Description | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| No website found | Business may be offline-only, informal, or new | Grade D, note "no web presence found" |
| No reviews | New business or niche market | Grade D, not E |
| Registration not checked | Registry not accessible or business too small | Note "registration not verified" |
| Contact not published | Business prefers WhatsApp/phone only | Grade D, note "contact not public" |
| Limited product info | Catalog not online | Grade D, request spec sheet |
| Single source mention | Found in one directory only | Grade D, note "single source" |

**Rule:** Grade E requires at least one **red flag**, not just missing evidence. Grade D is "we couldn't verify enough" — Grade E is "we found reasons to worry."

### 4.3 Decision table

| Situation | Grade | Wording |
|---|---|---|
| Strong positive evidence, no negatives | A | "Verified supplier with strong public evidence" |
| Moderate evidence, no negatives | B | "Supplier with moderate public evidence" |
| Mixed or minimal evidence | C | "Supplier with limited public evidence" |
| Almost nothing found | D | "Insufficient public evidence to verify" |
| Active red flags found | E | "Warning signals observed: [list]" |

## 5. COMPLAINT AND REVIEW ANALYSIS

### 5.1 Where to look

| Platform | What it shows | Bias |
|---|---|---|
| Google Reviews | Local business reputation | Mixed — real customers |
| Marketplace ratings | Transaction-based reviews | Mixed — verified purchases only |
| Trustpilot / Sitejabber | International review platforms | Mixed — self-selected reviewers |
| Social media comments | Customer interactions | Mixed — public complaints visible |
| Forum/Reddit | Community discussions | Mixed — anecdotal |
| Consumer protection | Government complaint records | Negative-skewed (only complaints filed) |

### 5.2 Review analysis method

1. **Volume check:** 0 reviews = insufficient data; 1–5 = anecdotal; 10+ = pattern emerging.
2. **Sentiment split:** Count positive vs negative vs neutral. Don't just read the average.
3. **Complaint themes:** Group negative reviews by issue (delivery, quality, communication, refund).
4. **Response pattern:** Does the business respond to negative reviews? How?
5. **Review authenticity:** Look for generic phrasing, same-date bursts, no-detail 5-stars.
6. **Recency:** Weight recent reviews more — businesses change.

### 5.3 Complaint severity classification

| Level | Description | Impact on grade |
|---|---|---|
| Isolated complaint | One unhappy customer, no pattern | Minimal — note it |
| Recurring theme | Multiple complaints about same issue | Moderate — downgrade one level |
| Serious allegations | Fraud, non-delivery, safety issues | Major — Grade E candidate |
| Verified scam reports | Confirmed by authorities or multiple sources | Critical — Grade E, exclude |

### 5.4 When reviews conflict with other evidence

- **Good registration + bad reviews:** Grade B/C with note "registration verified but customer complaints exist."
- **No registration + good reviews:** Grade C/D with note "positive reviews but business registration unverified."
- **All evidence positive but price suspiciously low:** Grade B with flag "price below market — verify quality."

## 6. CONFIDENCE REPORTING FORMAT

Every verification report must state confidence explicitly. Confidence ≠ trust grade.

| Confidence | Meaning | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| **HIGH** | Multiple independent sources confirm key facts | Registration verified + website + reviews + consistent info |
| **MODERATE** | Some sources confirm, some gaps | Website + contact verified, registration not checked |
| **LOW** | Minimal evidence, mostly self-reported | Only website or only directory listing |
| **INSUFFICIENT** | Almost nothing found | Single mention, no verifiable details |

### 6.1 Report format

```markdown
VERIFICATION REPORT: [Supplier Name]
Date: [YYYY-MM-DD]

CHECKS PERFORMED:
- Business registration: [found/not found/could not verify] — [source]
- Website: [URL/status] — [assessment]
- Contact info: [consistent/inconsistent/not published]
- Physical address: [verified/unverified/not found]
- Authorized status: [verified/unverified/not applicable]
- Reviews: [platform/count/sentiment summary]
- Red flags: [none/list]
- Gaps: [list of what couldn't be checked]

TRUST GRADE: [A/B/C/D/E]
CONFIDENCE: [HIGH/MODERATE/LOW/INSUFFICIENT]

SUMMARY: [1-2 sentences explaining the grade and confidence]
RECOMMENDATION: [proceed with caution / verify further / exclude / etc.]
```

### 6.2 Confidence vs. grade examples

| Grade | Confidence | Scenario |
|---|---|---|
| A | HIGH | Registered, authorized, reviewed, consistent |
| B | MODERATE | Real business, some verification gaps |
| C | LOW | Minimal footprint, self-reported only |
| D | INSUFFICIENT | Almost nothing found |
| E | HIGH | Red flags confirmed by multiple sources |

Note: Grade E can have HIGH confidence — you're confident there are warning signals. Grade A can have MODERATE confidence — strong evidence but not exhaustively verified.

## 7. VERIFICATION LIMITATIONS

State these when relevant:

- **Point-in-time:** Verification reflects public info on the access date. Businesses change.
- **Public-only:** Cannot verify private financials, actual capacity, or unlisted contacts.
- **Regional variance:** Registration systems differ by country; some informal businesses lack formal registration but are legitimate.
- **No contact made:** Engine does NOT call, email, or visit suppliers. Verification is desk-based only.
- **Language barriers:** Sources in local language may be missed if search is English-only.
- **Fake reviews possible:** Review platforms can be manipulated; cross-reference.
